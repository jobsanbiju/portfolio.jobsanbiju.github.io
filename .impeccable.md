## Design Context

### Users
This is a personal filmmaker portfolio for Job San Biju. The audience is intentionally broad: commercial clients, creative agencies, directors, producers, collaborators, festival or program reviewers, and general viewers who want to understand the work. Visitors may arrive with different levels of intent, from quickly judging the visual style to deciding whether to reach out for a project.

The main jobs are to watch the selected work, understand Job's cinematic voice, find enough trust signals to evaluate fit, contact him for a project, and optionally follow on Instagram.

### Brand Personality
The brand should feel cinematic, premium, intimate, raw, moody, human, experimental, elegant, and tactile. It should communicate craft through image, motion, pacing, and atmosphere rather than generic portfolio conventions.

The tone should be confident without feeling corporate, emotional without becoming sentimental, and polished without losing the personal, handmade quality of the current direction.

### Aesthetic Direction
The current design direction is creator-led and should be preserved: dark cinematic surfaces, hero video as the main first impression, warm red halation, filmic contrast, serif display typography, and immersive full-bleed media. Future changes should polish and refine this existing direction rather than replace it.

The site should not drift into a generic dark neon portfolio, corporate template layout, stock creative-agency aesthetic, or repetitive card-heavy design. Glow, grain, and atmosphere should feel motivated by filmmaking and optics, not decoration for its own sake.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with moving image and mood. The first viewport should immediately communicate cinematic craft.
2. Preserve the personal authorship of the design while improving polish, spacing, hierarchy, and responsiveness.
3. Make work easy to watch. The path from landing to viewing selected films should be obvious and low-friction.
4. Make contact easy without overexplaining. Clients and collaborators should quickly find email, Instagram, and the reason to reach out.
5. Use atmosphere with restraint. Halation, glow, overlays, and motion should support readability and tone, never compete with the work.
